Learning focus: hazard recognition, chemical information, hierarchy of controls, safe storage/transfer, spill readiness and appropriate PPE.

Glossary of terms

  • SDS — Safety Data Sheet: A document containing information about a chemical's hazards, safe handling, storage, exposure controls, PPE and emergency measures.
  • PPE — Personal Protective Equipment: Equipment worn to reduce exposure, such as safety glasses, gloves and protective clothing. PPE is one level in the hierarchy of controls.
  • Hierarchy of controls: A framework for controlling risk, prioritising elimination, substitution and engineering controls before administrative controls and PPE.
  • Local exhaust ventilation (LEV): An engineering control that captures airborne contaminants such as vapours close to where they are generated.
  • Volatile: Describes a substance that evaporates readily and can produce vapour in the air.
  • Flammable: Capable of catching fire readily under specified conditions.
  • Corrosive: Capable of causing severe damage to skin, eyes or materials through chemical action.
  • Compatible: Suitable for contact or storage together without creating an unacceptable chemical or physical hazard.
  • Secondary container: A container into which a chemical has been transferred from its original container for use or temporary storage.
  • Ignition source: A source of enough energy to ignite a flammable material, such as a spark, flame or hot surface.

Example Safety Data Sheet — Acetone (training extract)
HazardsHighly flammable liquid and vapour. Causes serious eye irritation. Vapours may cause drowsiness or dizziness.
PreventionKeep away from heat, sparks and ignition sources. Use adequate ventilation. Avoid breathing vapour.
PPEWear appropriate eye protection and chemical-resistant gloves selected for the task and exposure.
Spill responseRemove ignition sources if safe to do so, ventilate the area, contain/absorb the spill with compatible material and follow site procedures.
StorageKeep container tightly closed in a cool, well-ventilated place and store in an appropriate flammable-liquid storage area.

Training extract only. In a real workplace, always use the current SDS supplied for the specific chemical/product.

Brief SOP — Broken glass and chemical spill
  1. Stop work and isolate the area. Keep others away from the broken glass and spill.
  2. Do not pick up glass by hand. Use tongs, forceps, a brush and dustpan, or other suitable tools.
  3. Identify the spilled chemical and consult the SDS. Confirm hazards, required PPE and spill-response controls before cleanup.
  4. Control chemical-specific hazards. For a flammable solvent such as IPA, remove ignition sources if safe to do so and ensure suitable ventilation.
  5. Clean up safely. Place glass fragments in the designated broken-glass/sharps container. Manage the liquid with the appropriate spill kit and compatible absorbent material.
  6. Report and restore the area. Notify the relevant staff member, dispose of contaminated waste correctly, and make sure the bench is safe before work resumes.

Training SOP only. Follow the current Adelaide University/local laboratory SOP and emergency procedures where they differ.
